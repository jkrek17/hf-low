Composites: hurricane force lows against storm-force-only lows

← Back to the archive
ERA5 proxy composites, not direct observation. Every figure here is built from ERA5 reanalysis fields around cyclone tracks found in ERA5 itself (pipeline A), for the 2004-05 to 2025-26 seasons. An “HF low” here is a cyclone whose ERA5 wind fields look like the ones OPC warns for as hurricane force: the 800 km ocean gust index reaches 71.7 kt. It is a proxy, it has not been validated before the archive begins, and ERA5 reads low in the strongest storms. The archive on the other tabs is the record; these pages are research composites.
In plain words
  • What pattern comes before an HF low? A slightly deeper trough and stronger 250 hPa jet than before a storm-force-only low. In the Atlantic box (53°N, 41°W) 500 hPa heights are 7 m lower and the jet 1.1 m/s stronger over days −10 to −4, and 22 m lower and 1.9 m/s stronger on the onset day. The Pacific is weaker (6 m, 0.8 m/s). Sea-surface temperature does not differ.
  • What does an HF low look like against a storm-force low? Deeper (971 against 984 hPa Atlantic, 974 against 985 Pacific), with about 60% steeper pressure gradient, the strongest gust closer in (302 against 407 km Atlantic, 233 against 333 km Pacific) and a wider area of 48-kt gusts. At equal central pressure the wider wind area survives in the Atlantic (+39 km) and not in the Pacific.
  • When do they start to differ, and what is not different? The pressure gradient separates first, 36 to 48 hours before the peak; central pressure 12 to 24 hours before. The hemispheric pattern changes how HF lows look only weakly (Atlantic only, not robust). Among deepening storms, the larger-scale environment separates those that reach HF, but they are already deeper and older.

The typical pattern

The Atlantic pattern in absolute terms: 500 hPa height contours with 250 hPa wind in colour, and sea-level pressure, for HF onsets (top) and storm-force peaks (bottom).

The groups differ in age and in where the anchor sits (HF onsets are on average 2.6° of latitude further south than storm-force peaks in the Atlantic and 5.7° in the Pacific), and storms in the same week share an environment. The season (22 of them) is the independent sample, not the storm.

At the storm

Storm-scale composites at 0.25°

400 HF and 400 storm-force-only storms per basin, matched by month, rotated so right of motion is down. Columns: HF onset, HF peak, storm-force peak, and the two differences. Rows: 10 m gust, 10 m wind speed, sea-level pressure, 2 m dewpoint. Gust and wind differences are partly true by construction (HF is defined by the gust index).

Moisture caveat. HF storms look moister within 500 km, but the dewpoint is raw and not adjusted for latitude, and HF onsets sit further south (2.6° Atlantic, 5.7° Pacific). Do not read the dewpoint panels as moisture causing HF. 23 of 28 pre-registered structure tests pass. ERA5 under-resolves the strongest winds, so areas are biased low; shapes and HF-minus-storm-force differences are more trustworthy than absolute values.

Around every HF low

Upper-air composites of all HF lows

Every HF low from October to April, 2004 to 9 January 2023 (664 Atlantic, 559 Pacific), averaged around the storm centre and rotated so the direction of motion points right. Columns: 48, 24 and 12 hours before onset, onset and peak. Top row: the mean field; bottom row: the difference from the 1990–2019 climate. This is a descriptive average of ERA5 at 1.5° (a proxy, not direct observation), not a test.

Read with care. These are ERA5 proxy fields on a coarse 1.5° grid; they are not observations. The storm’s own circulation has not been removed, so the wind pattern near the centre (the weaker wind at the centre and part of the shift of the jet to behind the storm) is partly the storm itself, not the jet. An average smears jet streaks and troughs, so it cannot show entrance and exit regions, trough tilt or trough position. The 48-hour columns rest on the 151 Atlantic and 92 Pacific storms that already had a track then. Wind speed is the grid-point speed interpolated to the storm box. A fresh agent recomputed every number quoted here from the raw fields; the bootstrap intervals and the figure pixels were not rechecked. The 250 hPa divergence maps and the north-up views are in the repository under research/era5/hf_env_composites.
