Outlook replay: how past forecasts lined up with what OPC called

A replay of past forecasts, not a live forecast. Each map is a real GFS model run from 2021–2026, shaded the same way as the live outlook: blue shows how often, in past cases with a similar forecast, OPC (NOAA’s Ocean Prediction Center) had a strong low nearby. The diamonds are where OPC’s analysis actually put its strong lows, so you can see how well the shading matched. The shading uses data from the other four seasons only, never the one being shown.

What you are looking at

Blue shading shows where, in past cases with a forecast like this one, OPC had a strong low nearby. The darker the blue, the more often that happened:

  • Light blue: about 1 to 3 times in 10.
  • Medium blue: about 3 to 5 times in 10.
  • Dark blue: 5 times in 10 or more.
  • No shading: under 1 in 10. That is a lower chance, not a guarantee of calm.

How to use it: pick a past run with the arrows or the list, then a day (Day 1 is the first 24 hours), a basin and a strength. Hover or tap the map for the number at a spot. Diamonds are OPC’s own strong-low positions for that day: a good map has diamonds sitting inside the blue.

“OPC agreed” means OPC’s own analysis had a low of that strength within 324 nautical miles of the point on that day. Hurricane force is wind of 64 kt or more, storm force 48 to 63 kt.

Lookup card: hover or tap the map

Shows how often OPC agreed in past cases with a similar forecast gust near that point, from the other four seasons.

Other marks on the map (context only)

These come from the same GFS forecast, at the end of the day shown. They help you place the shading; they do not change it and have no percentages.

  • L: a low-pressure centre of 992 hPa or lower, with its pressure. Lows over high ground such as Greenland are left out, because their sea-level pressure is not real.
  • Orange dashed line: a dip (trough) in the winds about 5.5 km up (the 500 hPa level). It is a simple finder and is often the west side of a storm’s own upper-level low.
  • Purple: the core of the jet stream (winds of 110 kt or more at about 10 km, the 250 hPa level).
  • Hatched circle (about 500 km): a low where strong upper-level lift and a strong temperature contrast are both present, a setup that has gone with rapid strengthening. A hint only; see “How this works”.

Keep in mind

  • The shading uses the model’s forecast gusts as a stand-in for the sustained wind OPC warns on, and OPC’s hurricane-force call is an analyst’s judgement, sometimes made as a service to customers. So “OPC agreed” does not mean the wind was that strong.
  • No shading does not mean safe. It means that in the past, forecasts like this were followed by an OPC low less than 1 time in 10.
  • The percentages come from five past seasons of one model, so read them as rough rates, not exact odds. Distances are in nautical miles.
  • Only forecast days 1 to 4 and only October to April. There is no gale shading, because there is no OPC record of gale-force lows to measure against.

How well the shading lined up with OPC across the replays

Reading the table: of OPC’s hurricane-force low positions in these runs, the share that had blue shading of 15% or more (or 5% or more) within 324 nautical miles. The last column is how much of the ocean on the map was shaded at 15% or more, so you can see the shading is not just covering everything. Fixes outside the map view are left out. The groups are explained in “How this works” below.

How this works (details for the curious)

The shading

For each point on a 1-degree ocean grid we take the strongest GFS forecast gust within a set distance on that forecast day (60 nautical miles on Day 1, 120 on Day 2, 270 on Days 3 and 4: the smallest distances that caught at least 80% of hurricane-force gust area when the model low was out of place, on the ERA5 reanalysis proxy, position error only). We then look up how often, in the other four seasons, OPC had a hurricane-force or storm-force low fix within 324 nautical miles of points with that gust. Those shares are the percentages. The areas are drawn from the model’s own wind field, not from tracking a low, so a storm the tracker misses still shows.

Which runs are shown

Two groups, both fixed before looking at the results. The 20 picked runs: per season, the two runs with the most OPC hurricane-force fixes in the next 96 hours, one run where OPC called none but GFS gust was strongest, and one picked at random. The routine sample: every tenth 00 UTC run of the five seasons, nothing chosen for interest, so it is the fairer read of how often the shading covers OPC’s fixes. Each run is shaded with a table that leaves out its own season. A fix counts as covered when an ocean point shaded 15% or more (or 5% or more) lies within 324 nautical miles of it, the radius the percentages are built on.

The marks on the map

Lows are GFS pressure minima of 992 hPa or lower; the shading never uses them. The jet core is 250 hPa wind of 110 kt or more. The hatch marks a low whose surroundings have both strong 500 hPa vorticity advection (upper-level lift) and strong low-level baroclinicity (temperature contrast): the cut points are the upper thirds of ERA5 proxy fixes of 2004–2026 (pipeline A, chosen after looking), applied here to GFS, which was not tested for this display. Among all those ERA5 cyclone fixes, the next 24 h reached storm force or stronger 41% of the time with coupled surroundings against 8% with quiet ones; that is a different population and horizon from the lows drawn here, so read it as a hint, not a rate for this map.

Limits

Five seasons (October–April 2021–22 to 2025–26), 00 UTC runs only, one model that changed version inside the window. Gust is sampled every 6 hours, so a short peak can be missed. Points are 1 degree apart and in the same run they are not independent: the real sample is runs and seasons, so the percentages are rates, not precise odds, and fixes within one storm are not independent, and a run every 10th day still shares weather patterns with its neighbours. Land and coasts are not scored. Nothing beyond day 4 or outside October–April. The ERA5 record is a proxy, not direct observation.

Method and pre-registration: research/era5/outlook_replay/ in the repository (counts in results/point_days.csv). The shading radii come from a separate position-error test on the ERA5 proxy (research/era5/neighborhood_radius/).
