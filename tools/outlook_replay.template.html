Outlook replay: past GFS runs, shaded by how often OPC agreed

What we learned · ← Back to the archive
A replay of past forecasts, not a live forecast. Every map is a GFS run from 2021–2026 shaded with the table built from the other four seasons, with OPC’s actual low fixes drawn on top. GFS gust is a model forecast and a proxy for the sustained wind OPC warns on; OPC’s hurricane-force call is an analyst’s judgement, sometimes made as a service to customers, so “OPC agreed” is not the same as “the wind was there”.
In plain words
  • What the colours mean. Pick a point on the map. Look at the strongest GFS gust within a set distance of it during that forecast day (60 nautical miles on day 1, 120 on day 2, 270 on days 3 and 4). In past seasons, when that gust was in the same band, OPC had a hurricane-force low within 324 nautical miles of the point on the percentage the colour shows.
  • It is a neighbourhood, not one storm. The colours come straight from the GFS wind field, not from tracking a low, so a storm the tracker would have lost still shows.
  • No colour is not “safe”. Uncoloured ocean means GFS gust stayed in a band where OPC agreed under 1% of the time. It is a lower chance, not a guarantee, and the model can miss a storm.
  • Even the strongest gusts are not a sure thing. Where the GFS gust was 80 kt or more, OPC had a hurricane-force low nearby on about 58% of point-days on day 1 and about 39% by day 4. Chances fall with lead time.

How the shading lined up with OPC across the replays

Two groups, both fixed before looking at the results. The 20 picked runs: per season, the two runs with the most OPC hurricane-force fixes in the next 96 hours, one run where OPC called none but GFS gust was strongest, and one picked at random. The routine sample: every tenth 00 UTC run of the five seasons, nothing chosen for interest, so it is the fairer read of how often the shading covers OPC’s fixes. Each run is shaded with a table that leaves out its own season. A fix counts as covered when an ocean point shaded 15% or more (or 5% or more) lies within 324 nautical miles of it, the radius the percentages are built on. Fixes outside the map view are left out.

Limits. Five seasons (October–April 2021–22 to 2025–26), 00 UTC runs only, one model that changed version inside the window. Gust is sampled every 6 hours, so a short peak can be missed. Points are 1 degree apart and in the same run they are not independent: the real sample is runs and seasons, so the percentages are rates, not precise odds, and fixes within one storm are not independent, and a run every 10th day still shares weather patterns with its neighbours. Land and coasts are not scored. Nothing beyond day 4 or outside October–April.

Method and pre-registration: research/era5/outlook_replay/ in the repository (counts in results/point_days.csv). The shading radii come from a separate position-error test on the ERA5 proxy (research/era5/neighborhood_radius/).
